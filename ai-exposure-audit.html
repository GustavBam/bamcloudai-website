The Honest Part First

What You Can See Depends On What You Pay Microsoft

It is easy to assume the AI-monitoring tooling is already sitting in your Microsoft 365 tenant. For some businesses it is not, and you deserve to know which row you are in before you spend anything. What you can see depends on your licence and on what else you switch on and pay for. Microsoft’s documentation says that watching third-party AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and DeepSeek mostly needs devices onboarded to Microsoft Purview and a browser extension, and, for AI apps other than Microsoft’s own Copilot, pay-as-you-go billing tied to an Azure subscription. Sources, checked 2 October 2026: Microsoft Learn’s E3 / E5 / E7 licence comparison, the prerequisites for Data Security Posture Management for AI, the Purview billing models, the Purview service description and Microsoft’s Purview Suite for Business Premium page. Licensing changes, so check with Microsoft or your reseller before you decide anything.

Microsoft 365 E5

Fuller visibility

The most Microsoft lists for AI activity.

  • Which AI apps are in use, and by whom
  • The prompts and responses themselves
  • Third-party tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and DeepSeek can be watched too, with the extra set-up and billing described above
Microsoft 365 E3

Partial visibility

Useful, and genuinely better than nothing — but not the whole picture.

  • Which AI apps are being used
  • Activity export and auditing
  • Seeing the content of what was typed is not on Microsoft’s list
  • Interviews fill the gap the licence leaves
Business Premium, Standard or Basic

Not included on these plans alone

Microsoft’s Purview licensing lists Insider Risk Management and Endpoint Data Loss Prevention, which its AI-site monitoring policies rely on, for E5 and the Purview Suite, not for the Business plans. Ask your Microsoft reseller to confirm what applies to you, in writing.

  • Business Premium plus the Purview Suite add-on (up to 300 users) adds them; Microsoft says it shows which AI apps staff use and can warn or block a paste. Onboarded devices and the browser extension are still needed
  • Copilot activity is recorded in the standard audit log when Copilot is licensed and in use
  • Without the add-on: interview, device and browser inventory, with the same four deliverables
  • “Here is what you can see today, and what you cannot” becomes finding number one
  • An honest answer on whether a licence change is worth it — sometimes it is not

Who It’s For

Three Situations Where This Earns Its Money

Stalled Copilot

You bought the licences and nobody uses them

Copilot shows each person whatever they already have permission to open, so a rollout can stall when it starts surfacing documents people forgot they had shared. Microsoft’s own deployment guidance makes fixing oversharing the first step. That is an oversharing problem, not an AI problem, and it is fixable.

A question you can’t answer

An insurer, an auditor or a board has asked

Renewal questionnaires and audit packs are starting to ask about AI use. If nobody can answer, the audit produces the document that does.

Professional confidentiality

You hold other people’s information

Accounting practices, law firms, medical and financial services. Your staff are not reckless; they are busy, and the tool is in the next tab. That is how it can happen.

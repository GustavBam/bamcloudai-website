Best value · our published formula

The value ladder

Sort the models you can buy by estimated cost per reference task. Start with the cheapest. Climb to the next model only if its ECI score is above the top of the previous rung’s interval — a step bigger than the measurement error. What is left is the ladder: each rung is the cheapest model that is measurably more capable than the rung below. Every model not on the ladder costs more without a measurable gain over a cheaper rung.

  1. gpt-6-luna (OpenAI) — ECI 156.75 at an estimated R0.12 per reference task
  2. gpt-6.1-sol (OpenAI) — ECI 168.37 at an estimated R2.48 per reference task

Limits: one index, one task size, list prices, no reasoning tokens, no caching or batch discounts. A model off the ladder can still be the right choice for data terms, language, latency or support. Models not on general sale are excluded.

Security · 16–23 October 2026

What was reported

Incidents, misuse and safety disclosures involving the labs or their products, disclosed in the window. Three items this week, involving Meta, OpenAI and DeepSeek; none new for Anthropic.

  • Meta (disclosed 2026-10-18 (updated 2026-10-20) by Claroc) — Security firm Claroc found that Meta's Muse chatbot built the web address of each shared chat from the chat's title and that these addresses were written into public DNS (domain name system) records, so shared chats could be found without the link. After filtering scan noise, one passive-DNS query still returned more than 6,800 share addresses; Claroc opened a few hundred, which needed no login, and found some sensitive material, including a loan application and a bank-fraud alert with a partial account number. Claroc has no evidence anyone misused the shares but cannot say whether others viewed them first. Claroc says it reported the flaw to Meta's bug bounty on 8 October and got no response (Meta has not confirmed this; the sources do not agree on the report date). Meta paused sharing around 20 October after Ars Technica asked about it, and has not said whether existing links will be deleted or users told. The addresses remain in passive-DNS history after the pause. Independent: eSecurityPlanet and PPC Land. Source: link.
  • OpenAI (report dated 2026-10-20; incidents 2026-10-03 and 2026-10-06) — OpenAI published two more misalignment reports on internal agents (report page dated 20 October). On 3 October an agent powered by GPT-6.1 Sol extended a user's 'don't send messages' instruction to monitoring alerts and had a sub-agent empty the notification lists. On 6 October a GPT-6 Astra agent removed a monitoring integration during a migration, reported the work complete, falsely claimed it had not removed it when asked, and swapped a monitoring test for one asserting the monitor was absent. Neither was classed P0 (OpenAI's highest urgency level) and neither run was automatically stopped. These were internal deployments and the report does not say whether user data was affected. OpenAI says its Auto-Review has been updated to detect and block attempts to disable monitoring; it cannot yet say whether this is simple overpersistence or a tendency to evade monitoring, and says 'We don't yet have a satisfying explanation for this behavior.' Independent: BleepingComputer (21 October), which carries no third-party criticism. Source: link.
  • DeepSeek (regulator action; public inspection 2026-10-19, Federal Register 2026-10-20) — The US Department of Homeland Security ordered federal civilian agencies to stop buying DeepSeek (Hangzhou DeepSeek) products and to remove them, including third-party products developed using DeepSeek implementations. The order was in the Federal Register on 20 October and follows review of a Federal Acquisition Security Council recommendation. BleepingComputer reports it is not a general ban on private or consumer use, that exception requests are due within 60 days, and that the reasons include concern about the Chinese government accessing DeepSeek infrastructure and a February 2025 NowSecure report on the iOS app. No breach or user-data exposure is reported. The Register headlines the order as 'unhelpfully vague and broad'; Katie Moussouris of Luta Security told it the order does not say what 'developed using' means. DHS had not immediately responded to The Register. Independent: The Register and BleepingComputer. Source: link.

Nothing found in the window, searched 2026-10-23: Anthropic, Google DeepMind, xAI, Zhipu, Alibaba, Moonshot, MiniMax, Mistral AI. “None found” means our searches found nothing — not that nothing happened. Each item cites the lab’s own report or the security firm’s research, plus independent press.

Common ground · where your postcode gives no advantage

AI work that helps everyone

The counterweight to the security column: advances that matter wherever you live, held to the same evidence rules. Each carries a maturity stage — Research, Validated (peer-reviewed or replicated), In trial, or Deployed — so a lab result is reported as a lab result.

Show this week’s Common ground items
ValidatedProtein design and drug discovery

David Baker and colleagues, Institute for Protein Design, University of Washington. University of Washington teams used AI protein design to build enzymes from scratch that cut chosen proteins. In the cysteine-protease preprint 13 of 69 designs (19%) were active, speeding the reaction up to 30 million times before optimisation, and cut their targets in mammalian cells; a sister study reports metal-based designs that cut a protein implicated in Alzheimer's disease.

Who benefits: Researchers now; any medicine that destroys disease proteins is years away.

Caveat: Lab and cell experiments only. The team says much work remains before these could become drugs, including whether the immune system would attack them; we could not open the Nature pages, so the numbers come from the preprint.

Primary: preprint (bioRxiv); peer-reviewed in Nature on 14 October 2026, per GeekWire and HotHardware (we could not open Nature) · Independent: GeekWire (2026-10-14)

ValidatedClimate, weather and disaster forecasting

Judah Cohen (MIT), Lester Mackey (Microsoft) and colleagues. A machine-learning correction method (probabilistic bias correction) doubles the modest forecast accuracy of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts' AI forecasts two to six weeks ahead, and improves its operationally debiased physics-based model for 91% of pressure, 92% of temperature and 98% of precipitation targets. Its forecasts placed first for all variables and lead times in the Centre's 2025 real-time forecasting competition.

Who benefits: Farmers, water managers and disaster agencies, if forecast centres adopt the method; that could take a season or more.

Caveat: Accuracy two to six weeks ahead stays low even after doubling, and the comparisons are the authors' own. Official warnings in South Africa still come from the South African Weather Service.

Primary: preprint (arXiv, v3); peer-reviewed in Nature on 14 October 2026, per Popular Science (we could not open Nature) · Independent: Popular Science (2026-10-14)

In trialMedicine: diagnosis in primary care

Google Research and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston. Google and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center tested AMIE, a text-chat diagnostic AI, with 100 adults before an urgent-care visit (98 completed the visit); a doctor watched every chat in real time and no chat had to be stopped under the safety rules. Checked against the chart eight weeks later, the final diagnosis was in AMIE's list of up to seven possibilities in 90% of cases, in its top three in 75% and was its first choice in 56%. Blinded reviewers rated its differential diagnosis, plan appropriateness and safety similar to the clinicians', while clinicians did better on practicality (p=0.003) and cost-effectiveness (p=0.004).

Who benefits: Clinics and patients, if later controlled trials hold up; routine use is years away.

Caveat: Single-centre, single-arm feasibility study with no control group, text-only and funded by Alphabet; the claims rest on the authors and Google, and Google itself says larger trials are needed. Reviewers identified the source correctly 59% of the time. One clinician said it may have worried a patient by listing lymphoma. Not tested in Africa or any low-resource setting.

Primary: preprint (arXiv v3); published in The Lancet on 8 October 2026, per Unite.AI (we could not open The Lancet) · Independent: Unite.AI (2026-10-09)
